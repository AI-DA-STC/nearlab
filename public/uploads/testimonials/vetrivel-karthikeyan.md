I joined AI.R STC at ST Engineering as an AI & Robotics Intern from May to July 2026. During the three-month internship, I had the opportunity to contribute to a robotics project that explored how reinforcement learning could be used to train the Unitree B2W, a wheeled quadruped robot, to navigate challenging environments.

The internship was initially challenging because I felt that I was expected to already be familiar with every aspect of the project. However, my supervisor provided strong guidance by sharing relevant resources and patiently explaining concepts that I was uncertain about. More importantly, he gave me the freedom to explore different approaches and learn through experimentation. While he established clear expectations for my deliverables, he trusted me to determine the best way to achieve them. This balance between guidance and independence helped me become more confident, resourceful, and willing to take ownership of my work.

What stood out most to me about the culture at the branch was how welcoming and open everyone was. Even colleagues from different departments or those who were not directly involved in my project were willing to share their knowledge and experiences. This created a comfortable environment where I could approach others, ask questions about their work, and gain exposure to the wide range of projects being carried out within the branch.

I particularly enjoyed the academic huddles, where graduates and PhD students presented the projects and research they were working on. These sessions introduced me to topics beyond the scope of my own project and gave me a deeper appreciation of how research is conducted and communicated.

Overall, this internship gave me valuable exposure to the fields of robotics and artificial intelligence, while allowing me to develop both technically and professionally. Most importantly, it strengthened my interest in Physical AI and encouraged me to pursue further opportunities in this field.
